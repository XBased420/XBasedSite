The observed directly comparable single-page offers are about **$797–$1,200**, and Taylormade’s named business tiers are **$2,000–$3,500**. Broader low-end providers advertise less; those should not be merged into a single “market rate.” Care in this small sample runs **$50–$199/month**. Fifty-percent deposits appear in multiple offers. I did **not** find enough like-for-like first-party DFW prices to claim a reliable standalone booking-add-on range.

For a later decision, I would consider a tightly scoped first-three-client offer around **$350–$500 one-page**, **$900–$1,200 multi-page**, **$250–$400 booking-request add-on**, **$100/month bounded maintenance**, and **50% deposit**. These are my recommendations, not prices found in a survey. Specify page count, revision count, who supplies content, and included maintenance time before changing numbers.

At $100, a one-page project taking 10 hours produces $10/hour before expenses and tax. That makes the current one-page price a deliberate portfolio investment, rather than an obvious sustainable default. Keep a real boundary around the first three clients if you choose that strategy; do not invent a deadline or fake scarcity on the site. Around month six, consider $750–$1,000 one-page and $1,500–$2,000 multi-page only after reviewing real time spent, lead quality, and completed work. A calendar date alone is not a reason to raise prices.
